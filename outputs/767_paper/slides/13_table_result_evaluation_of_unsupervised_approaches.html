Table 2 evaluates the performance of various unsupervised approaches in identifying at least one helpful sentence from an email thread, which is crucial for enriching the context of a To-Do item generated from a commitment sentence. The metrics report the proportion of emails where at least one helpful sentence appears among the top K retrieved sentences (K=2 and K=3). Among the methods tested—Term Frequency (Tf), FastText with mean and max pooling, and pre-trained and fine-tuned BERT—the FastText (Max) approach achieves the highest scores: 0.85 at K=2 and 0.92 at K=3. This indicates that max-pooling over FastText embeddings effectively captures relevant contextual information. The results highlight that even simple embedding-based methods outperform more complex models like BERT when no labeled data is used for supervision, underscoring the effectiveness of the proposed unsupervised relevance scoring strategy.
